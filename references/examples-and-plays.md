# Examples & Situation-Specific Plays

## Salary / Job Offer
- **BATNA**: Other offers, staying in current role, freelancing runway.
- **Matrix**: Usually Collaborate or Compromise (relationship + ongoing employment matters).
- **Interests**: Compensation, growth, flexibility, title, impact — not just base salary.
- **Play**: Expand the package (bonus, equity, remote days, learning budget) to enlarge ZOPA. Use calibrated questions: “How can we structure this so it works for both the budget and my contribution?”
- **Voss moves**: Label their constraints (“It seems like headcount budgets are tight this year…”). Mirror key phrases about “fairness” or “market”.

## Vendor / Supplier Contract
- **BATNA**: Alternative suppliers or in-house option.
- **Matrix**: Compete if pure price, Collaborate if strategic long-term supply.
- **Play**: Trade volume commitments or longer term for better unit price / SLAs. Consider Ackerman if price is the main lever.
- **Voss**: “How am I supposed to accept that price given the volume we’re bringing?”

## Partnership / Joint Venture
- **Matrix**: Strongly Collaborate.
- **Play**: Map both parties’ strategic interests first (IFORESAW Interests + Who). Create joint upside mechanisms.
- **Voss**: Heavy labeling and calibrated questions to surface hidden concerns early.

## Conflict / Dispute Resolution
- **Matrix**: Often Collaborate or Accommodate depending on future relationship value.
- **Play**: Separate people from problem early. Use objective criteria. Lead with tactical empathy before any problem-solving.
- **Voss core**: Mirror + label emotions first; only then move to interests and options.

## One-time High-Stakes Deal (e.g. asset sale)
- **Matrix**: Compete (outcome-focused).
- **Play**: Strong BATNA is everything. Anchor high (or low if buying) but leave room inside ZOPA. Ackerman pattern often useful. Prepare walk-away language.
- **Voss**: Accusation audit of their likely suspicions about you.

## Internal Resource Negotiation (budget, headcount)
- **Interests**: Organizational goals, risk, timeline, political capital.
- **Play**: Frame requests in terms of company priorities (Harvard). Offer options that reduce the other side’s risk.
- **Voss**: “What would need to be true for this headcount to feel like a safe bet for you?”

## Sample Calibrated Questions (Voss)
- “What does success look like for you in this agreement?”
- “What constraints are you working under?”
- “How am I supposed to do that?”
- “What’s the biggest challenge you face on your side?”
- “How can we solve this in a way that works for both of us?”
- “What about this is most important to you?”
- “How would you like to proceed from here?”

## Sample Labels
- “It seems like you’re under pressure on timeline…”
- “It sounds like budget is the real constraint here…”
- “It looks like trust from previous experiences is a factor…”

## Expanding the ZOPA
- Add variables (payment terms, scope, exclusivity, support, future options, non-monetary items).
- Trade low-cost/high-value items.
- Introduce contingent agreements (“if X happens, then Y”).
- Bring in objective standards both sides accept.
- Use precise final numbers + a small non-monetary throw-in (Ackerman).

## Ackerman Quick Reference (when quantitative)
1. Target price set.
2. Open ≈ 65% of target.
3. Planned raises: 85% → 95% → 100%.
4. Empathy + calibrated questions between each move; never bid against yourself.
5. Final number is precise (not round).
6. Last offer includes a low-value non-monetary item to signal limit.
