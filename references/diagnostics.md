# Diagnostics & Common Mistakes

## Quick Diagnostic Questions

1. Have you clearly defined your BATNA *before* this conversation?
2. Do you know your reservation point (absolute walk-away)?
3. Have you estimated the other side’s BATNA and reservation point?
4. Are you negotiating positions or underlying interests?
5. Which quadrant of the Negotiation Matrix does this situation fall into?
6. Is there a clear ZOPA, or are you forcing a deal outside it?
7. Did you spend enough time in Information Exchange before Bargaining?
8. Will the relationship be stronger or weaker after this agreement?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Jumping to Bargaining** — Skipping Preparation and Information Exchange. Results in suboptimal or damaged deals.
- **No BATNA** — Feeling trapped, accepting bad terms, or appearing desperate.
- **Position-focused** — “I need X” instead of “I need X because…” → zero-sum fights.
- **Wrong strategy for the relationship** — Competing when collaboration is needed (or vice versa).
- **Ignoring ZOPA** — Pushing for terms outside the possible zone and killing the deal.
- **Winning the deal, losing the relationship** — Short-term gain that destroys future opportunities.
- **Talking more than listening** — Preparing scripts instead of questions.

## Red Flags

- “I just need them to agree to my number.”
- “I don’t have any alternatives if this falls through.”
- “We don’t have time to prepare — just go in and negotiate.”
- “It’s a one-time deal so relationship doesn’t matter” (often false).
- Measuring success only by how much you “got” rather than mutual value created.

## Interventions

- Force a written BATNA + reservation point before any meeting.
- Run a 15-minute “interests inventory” (list 5 underlying needs on each side).
- Explicitly choose the Negotiation Matrix strategy and stick to it.
- Prepare 8–10 open questions for the Information Exchange phase.
- After the deal, debrief: “Is the relationship stronger?”
