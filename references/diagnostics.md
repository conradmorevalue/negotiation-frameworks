# Diagnostics & Common Mistakes

## Quick Diagnostic Questions

1. Have you completed (or at least sketched) an IFORESAW-style preparation?
2. Have you clearly defined your BATNA *and* reservation point before this conversation?
3. Have you estimated the other side’s BATNA and reservation point?
4. Are you negotiating positions or underlying interests?
5. Which quadrant of the Negotiation Matrix does this situation fall into?
6. Is there a clear ZOPA, or are you forcing a deal outside it?
7. Did you spend enough time in Information Exchange (with real curiosity) before Bargaining?
8. Have you planned mirrors, labels, or calibrated questions?
9. Will the relationship be stronger *and* the agreement actually implementable after this?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Jumping to Bargaining** — Skipping Preparation and Information Exchange. Results in suboptimal or damaged deals.
- **No BATNA / weak alternatives** — Feeling trapped, accepting bad terms, or appearing desperate.
- **Position-focused** — “I need X” instead of “I need X because…” → zero-sum fights.
- **Wrong strategy for the relationship** — Competing when collaboration is needed (or vice versa).
- **Ignoring ZOPA** — Pushing for terms outside the possible zone and killing the deal.
- **Winning the deal, losing the relationship or implementation** — Short-term gain that fails in practice or destroys future opportunities.
- **Talking more than listening / persuading instead of understanding** — Preparing scripts instead of questions and labels.
- **Treating negotiation as natural talent** — Skipping frameworks and preparation.

## Red Flags

- “I just need them to agree to my number.”
- “I don’t have any alternatives if this falls through.”
- “We don’t have time to prepare — just go in and negotiate.”
- “It’s a one-time deal so relationship doesn’t matter” (often false).
- Measuring success only by how much you “got” rather than mutual value + implementability.
- Going in without any planned calibrated questions or labels.

## Interventions

- Force a written BATNA + reservation point + quick IFORESAW before any meeting.
- Run a 15-minute “interests inventory” (list 5 underlying needs on each side).
- Explicitly choose the Negotiation Matrix strategy and stick to it.
- Prepare 6–10 calibrated questions + 2–3 labels for the Information Exchange phase.
- After the deal, debrief: “Is the relationship stronger? Can both sides actually implement this?”
- For price talks, decide in advance whether Ackerman pattern is appropriate.
